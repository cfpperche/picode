## What the browser proved

Scratch instance, 1600 × 1200, two browser sessions. Eight panels — an interactive agent's TUI, a managed agent and six shells — plus a 24-panel matrix. The full row-by-row table is in `docs/architecture/matrix.md` (*Accepted in a browser*). **The pointer rule, proved rather than assumed:** at **100 %** a real click aimed at cell (25, 13) of a 29 × 16 pane came back from the terminal itself as `^[[<0;25;13M` — through tmux `mouse on`, the shipped default. At **83 %** xterm's own mapping for the same point answers **(21, 11)** — 4 columns and 2 rows out — the body reads `pointer-events: none`, the click produces **no** terminal report at all and moves the plane to 100 %, and the next click reports (25, 13) again. This is the behaviour the phase exists to protect.

**Budgets.** Marquee: 24 nodes selected at 61.9 fps, dragging all 24 at **62.7 fps** (worst frame 16.8 ms) in **one** PATCH of 24 rows, every rectangle moved by exactly (25, 19) units. Pan: 3 883 px at 4 678 px/s, **61.4 fps**. Chunk loading: 7 attaches and 7 tmux clients at rest → 0 attached / 7 suspended / 7 instances kept after the pan → the same 7 back; zoom 0.2 → 0 attaches → back to 1.0 → the same 7. Mode switch with six live panels, grid → canvas → grid: **zero** socket transitions, disposals or xterm replacements (polled at 40 ms). Maximize on a canvas refits 29 × 16 → 120 × 58 and tmux follows. ## Fixed here

- **Maximize on a canvas killed the pane it was showing.** A React Flow node's data is applied in an effect, so the maximize layer's body mounts one commit *before* the wrapper's unmounts; `releasePane`'s one-tick wait only covered the grid's order, so the wrapper's unmount suspended a socket the layer had already claimed (`readyState` 3, keystrokes lost). `paneOwnership.js` now counts mounted bodies: only the last host out parks the pane. Grid mode unchanged.
- **A body that is not a terminal was pointer-inert with them.** At 83 % the managed agent's *Open* sat under the snap layer and zoomed instead of opening. `hasPane` gates the pointer-events rule and the layer; a name-plate keeps the layer at any binding, because below 40 % the plate is the whole panel. The ⋯ tooltip also named only rename and delete while the menu offers Tidy on a canvas.
- **The minimap could not say where you were.** React Flow's two stroke widths are SVG user units — *plane* pixels — so the minimap scales them down with everything else: measured in dark, the viewport frame came out at 1.3:1 against the ground and the seam between two touching panels at 1.42:1, and the mask cannot carry the frame instead (45 % black over a near-black panel is 1.08:1). `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` on the mask and the nodes makes both screen-space at any plane size; verified in both themes.
- Two more from the captures: the snap pill was 90 % opaque, so the terminal read through its own explanation; and the library's badge stuck out half-eaten under a maximized panel. The pill is opaque and a covered canvas hides its badge. The `−`/`+` glyphs also read fainter than the words beside them (4.8:1 against 6.7:1 — one hairline glyph, same colour, fewer pixels) and now have a size of their own.

## Debts

`+` / `-` / `0` fire from the surface's roving tab stop, so a panel must be selected first — the guide says so; there is no document-level listener. A still stamps its age only when the feed has seen the panel change state since, so a frozen `top` shows none (verified: correct by design, invisible to a plain shell). React Flow's attribution badge is kept — removing it is a licence question, not a styling one. No docs-shots capture yet (needs a `desktop-matrix` profile and a fixture). The scratch daemon leaves `tmux: client` zombies: three of seven sessions still listed a client 25 s after their socket was suspended, never more than one per session, and production shows the same pattern — not the canvas's.
